What This Gift Actually Is

In true “Europe Through the Back Door” fashion, this isn’t a pre-packaged tour. It’s a toolkit for your own adventure.

  • Round-trip flights to Europe or Japan
    Find economy flights with good routes and reasonable fares so your budget stretches as far as possible. You might find that flying back from a different city gives you more options with your time. Consider flying into Madrid and back from Rome, or start in Berlin and make your way over to Lisbon. Major cities tend to have cheaper fares.
  • A long-distance travel pass
    In Europe, that probably means a train pass (Eurail or similar) — your magic carpet across the continent. Trains are the back-door traveler’s best friend: no airport hassle, views for days, and a front-row seat to how Europe actually works. Get a pass that will let you travel the entire time you are away: changing your plans mid trip is part of the fun, and a railpass makes this a fun option instead of a complicated headache. In Japan, it might be a mix of rail passes and buses that link your chosen cities.
  • Your budget is $3,500 each
    This total covers everything: flights, your pass, beds, bread, ramen, gelato, and the occasional museum that doesn’t bore you to tears. Pick your flights, your travel pass, and the remainder will be divided as a weekly stipend paid at the start of each week while you are away.

In other words: if you travel like the back door philosophy teaches — light, flexible, and a little bit frugal — this should get you roughly three to four weeks of real backpacking.

Door One: Europe — The Classic Back Door Journey

You already know Europe a bit — you’ve seen it with me in London, Switzerland, Venice, Paris, and Rome. Choosing Europe now means seeing it your way: picking the next city yourselves, navigating the stations, finding your own favorite café, and discovering the side streets that never show up in anyone’s highlight reel.

  • How you’ll move: mostly by train and tram — the original back door combo — choosing routes that make sense for your budget and curiosity.

Door Two: Japan — Back Door Travel in a Different Key

Japan is a place where tradition and ultra-modern life share the same block: quiet temples and tiny shrines, then neon and arcades five minutes away. It’s full of details — design, food, neighborhoods, fashion — that reward slow wandering and curiosity.

If you’re drawn to manga, anime, street style, design, or just the idea of exploring a place that feels completely different from anywhere else, Japan can be an incredible back door trip. It’s the kind of place where a side street can become the highlight of the day.

  • What it feels like: calm mornings, bright evenings, and a sense that even everyday life has been thoughtfully crafted.
  • How you’ll move: a mix of intercity trains, regional passes, and buses linking cities like Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka and beyond, plus local trains and subways that get you everywhere.
  • Why you might choose it: you want a trip built around culture, creativity, food, and wandering cities that feel like worlds of their own.

How Far Can $3,500 Take You?

If you chase luxury, $3,500 is a long weekend. If you travel like a backpacker, it’s a serious, memory-making chunk of time. Think of this as a challenge:

  • Sleep simple. Hostels, budget guesthouses, and the occasional cheap hotel or room-share. You’re there to sleep, not to admire the curtains.
  • Eat like a local. In Europe: street food, bakeries, grocery stores, picnic dinners, and the odd treat meal when something looks irresistible. In Japan: konbini snacks, ramen shops, curry houses, supermarket bentos, and tiny counter restaurants.
  • Move smart. Trains, trams, buses, your feet. Taxis and Ubers are the emergency-only button.
  • Pick a handful of bases. Fewer cities might give you more depth. Sometime it’s better to really know five places than to sprint through fifteen - but follow your curiousity.

With that mindset, your budget becomes less “Can we afford this?” and more “What’s the smartest, most interesting way to do this?”

Back Door Travel Rules (a.k.a. How to Make This Work)

To stretch this gift into three or four weeks, you’ll want to lean into the same principles that shaped my own first trip. Consider these your unofficial house rules:

  • Pack light. If you can’t comfortably carry your bag up a flight of stairs, it’s too heavy. You’ll thank yourself on day three.
  • Be flexible. Plans change. Trains run late. Some places feel “meh” and others feel like home. Adjust as you go.
  • Talk to people. Chat with hostel staff, other travelers, and locals. Most of your favorite memories will come from conversations.
  • Spend on experiences, not things. Your luggage space and your budget are both limited. Save them for memories, not souvenirs.
  • Accept a little discomfort. Early trains, occasional noise, slightly questionable bunk beds — they’re all part of the story you’ll tell later.
  • Be adventerous, not a headline
    Europe and Japan are full of thrills - mountains to climb, rivers to raft, cliffs to gaze over. Dot it all! Just do it smart. Guided adventures? Perfect! Helmet on, rope clipped, go make stories. But free-climbing, cliff-jumping, or swimming past the "No Entry" signs? That's a hard no. If you're tempted to try something that wild, remember, your can always come back on your own dime, but you can't do it on this trip.

Here’s the fine print — the friendly kind:

It’s about discovering that “normal life” looks completely different from place to place — and that you can belong in more of those places than you think.

A good backpacking trip quietly changes how you see the world — and how you see yourselves in it. I want you to have the chance to feel that: navigating on your own, the thrill of finding a perfect café by accident, the weird joy of sharing a kitchen with strangers from six different countries.
